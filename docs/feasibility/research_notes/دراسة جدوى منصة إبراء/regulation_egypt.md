## 1. ETA e-invoice and e-receipt: who must comply, phases, POS technical requirements, penalties, vendor accreditation

### Takeaway
B2B/B2G e-invoicing has been mandatory for all registered taxpayers for some time. The B2C e-receipt is mandatory only for taxpayers named in ETA decisions, but every scheduled phase (latest Decision 361/2025, 15 Nov 2025; other governorates 31 Dec 2025) has now passed. Also, the Law 6/2025 small-business regime requires joining e-invoice/e-receipt. In practice, any VAT-registered or simplified-regime shop that buys a POS in 2026 should expect to need e-receipt integration. For a SaaS POS this is a core feature to build, not an optional add-on. Software can be registered as a "virtual POS" through the ERP route. Formal ETA vendor certification exists and is a strong sales advantage, but I could not confirm that the law requires it for software-only vendors (CONFIRM with ETA).

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis and scope**
- E-receipt legal basis: arts. 35 and 37 of the Unified Tax Procedures Law No. 206/2020, Ministerial Decision 230/2022 (in force 26 May 2022) as amended by Decision 38/2024, and decisions of the Head of ETA that name the obliged taxpayers. — [NPI Egypt (Nassef & Partners), reviewed 9 Oct 2026](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- The obligation applies only once an ETA decision names the taxpayer. Lists are published on the ETA site, and an ETA inquiry service shows who is covered. Any taxpayer may register voluntarily before its date. — [NPI Egypt, 9 Oct 2026](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- B2B transactions take an e-invoice (whatever the value), not a receipt. E-invoicing began Nov 2020 with 134 large taxpayers and was extended to all remaining taxpayers by Ministerial Decision 323/2022 (Dec 2022). From 1 Dec 2022 government entities may only pay against ETA e-invoices. — [DDD Invoices, 19 Oct 2026](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-egypt); [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)

**Phases and dates (e-receipt)**
- Phase 1: 1 Jul 2022 (Decision 289/2022, 153 companies). Phase 2: 1 Oct 2022 (400 taxpayers, Greater Cairo/Alex). Phase 3: 15 Jan 2023 (2,000 taxpayers). Phase 4: 15 Apr 2023 to 15 Jul 2024 (health, restaurants, hotels, retail chains, mall stores). Phase 5: 15 Sep 2024 (culture, entertainment, education). Phase 6: restaurants/cafés outside malls, 15 Nov 2024 (Cairo/Alex) and 15 Jan 2025 (other governorates). Phase 7: health, personal care, telecoms, 1 Jul 2025 (Decision 123/2025). Phase 8: app transport, maintenance, cleaning, 15 Jul / 15 Sep 2025 (Decisions 225 and 281/2025). **Phase 9: supermarkets and other activities, 15 Nov 2025 for Greater Cairo and Alexandria (Decision 361/2025); 31 Dec 2025 for other governorates per the Minister's schedule.** — [NPI Egypt, 9 Oct 2026](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- "As at 9 October 2026 every date in the schedule has passed." Decision 361/2025 is the latest on ETA's list. No decision yet names the taxpayers of the second sub-phase of phase 9, and no later phase exists. — [NPI Egypt, 9 Oct 2026](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt); consistent with [DDD Invoices fiscalization guide, 16 Oct 2026](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt)
- **UNVERIFIED claim:** DDD Invoices says Egypt "reduced the mandatory e-invoicing and e-receipt registration threshold to EGP 250,000 in annual revenue, requiring compliance by March 31, 2026", citing Decision 281/2025. NPI describes 281/2025 as a phase-8 sector decision, and I found no other source for the EGP 250k threshold. Treat this as doubtful until checked on eta.gov.eg. — [DDD Invoices, 19 Oct 2026](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-egypt); conflicting characterisation in [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- ETA confirmed in March 2026 that businesses using the Law 6/2025 simplified regime must comply with e-invoice and e-receipt to keep the regime. — [DDD Invoices, 19 Oct 2026](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-egypt); the condition is in Law 6/2025 art. 3 per [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt) and [EY, 28 Feb 2025](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0577-egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises)
- New: a "temporary tax card" introduced by **Law No. 150 of 2026** cannot be used to issue receipts (art. 27 bis). — [NPI Egypt, 9 Oct 2026](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt) (single source; CONFIRM)

**Technical requirements for a POS (what our product must do)**
- Taxpayer registration, integration from an ERP or POS, item coding with GS1 or EGS, and real-time submission on the live environment. — [NPI Egypt citing Decision 230/2022 art. 2](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- Each POS is registered under "Points of sale" in the taxpayer's ETA profile. It gets a client ID/secret and must be **activated before submitting**. Only taxpayers with the B2C tag can submit receipts. — [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- A physical device is not always required. Receipts can come from a POS, an ERP, or ETA's mobile solution. **An ERP can be registered as a virtual POS**, and each sales point needs its own registration. — [DDD Invoices fiscalization, 16 Oct 2026](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt) citing [ETA e-Receipt FAQ v24, Jan 2024](https://eta.gov.eg/sites/default/files/2024-02/ERECEIPT-FAQ-V24-31-1-2024.pdf)
- API details: OAuth 2.0 authentication and JSON receipts through the eReceipt API. Batches hold up to 500 receipts (1.5 MB), each with up to 300 lines. The current document type is Receipt v1.2 (v1.0 and v1.1 retired). It needs the seller registration number, branch, POS serial, activity code, item codes and values. — [DDD Invoices fiscalization](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt); [ETA SDK receipt v1.2](https://sdk.invoicing.eta.gov.eg/documents/receipt-v1-2/)
- Each receipt carries a taxpayer-generated SHA-256 UUID plus `previousUUID`, which chains it to the previous receipt from the same POS. The printed receipt must show a QR code. The buyer's national ID (or a passport for foreigners) is required at EGP 150,000 or more (v1.2+). — [DDD Invoices](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-egypt); [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- Submission must happen within 24 hours of issue. Receipts 24 h to 30 days old go through the portal's late-submission request (capped per year, reason required). Offline handling means storing receipts locally with the UUID chain and syncing within 24 h. — [NPI Egypt citing ETA FAQ v24](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt); [DDD Invoices fiscalization](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt)
- B2B e-invoices need JSON/XML, an **electronic signature/seal** (USB token / e-seal certificate), GS1/EGS codes, and status handling (accepted, rejected, cancelled). — [DDD Invoices](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-egypt); [ETA SDK submit documents](https://sdk.invoicing.eta.gov.eg/einvoicingapi/01-submit-documents/)
- Since Decision 702/2023, phase decisions also require registration on ETA's consumer incentive programme ("Your invoice, your protection and your prize"). — [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)

**POS registration / vendor certification**
- The official ETA POS portal (pos.eta.gov.eg) offers "Taxpayers registration", a Vendor user guide, a POS model dropdown (hardware models such as PAX A920 Pro, Sunmi, Verifone X990, Elanda Windows/Android models), and a serial-number check. Its listing also mentions a "POS application / vendor certification process" and "Rules and criteria for accepting the certified companies". — [ETA POS portal, accessed 19 Nov 2026](https://pos.eta.gov.eg/en)
- "Devices supplied by an ETA-approved vendor can be registered directly while other devices must first undergo a technical inspection." — [The Invoicing Hub](https://www.theinvoicinghub.com/einvoicing-compliance-egypt/) (snippet); same in [DDD Invoices fiscalization](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt) and [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt) ("a point of sale is approved after the device is inspected")
- Competitor Daftra says ETA has approved several technology providers whose POS solutions can issue compliant receipts, including Daftra. — [Daftra hub](https://www.daftra.com/en/hub/egypt-electronic-receipt) (vendor self-claim)
- "Egypt does not use certified fiscal printers. ETA approves POS suppliers and models, and validates each receipt centrally." — [DDD Invoices fiscalization, 16 Oct 2026](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt)

**Penalties and knock-on effects**
- Breaching arts. 35 and 37 brings a fine of **EGP 20,000 to 100,000** (art. 71, Law 206/2020). ETA also states that not issuing can amount to tax evasion. — [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt); [DDD Invoices citing ETA](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt)
- The buyer's costs are deductible only if supported by e-invoices or e-receipts (receipts from Jan 2025, with a possible extension of up to one year, so at the latest Jan 2026), under Income Tax Law art. 22(2) as amended by Law 30/2023. — [NPI Egypt](https://npiegypt.com/articles/guides/e-receipt-egypt)
- Law 7/2025 amended the Unified Tax Procedures Law (penalty provisions). Law 5/2025 lets unregistered businesses regularise without fines. — [DDD Invoices fiscalization](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/fiscalization-in-egypt)

### Inferences
- Mandatory for us, commercially: e-receipt (B2C) integration is effectively mandatory to sell POS to Egyptian shops in 2026. Supermarkets and grocery shops (a core target) fell into phase 9 in Nov/Dec 2025, and simplified-regime shops must join. Shops that are not VAT- or tax-registered at all (informal micro shops) are outside it today, but a product without e-receipt locks us out of every formal shop.
- Build scope: ETA taxpayer onboarding wizard (client ID/secret per POS), virtual-POS registration per register/branch, GS1/EGS item code mapping, Receipt v1.2 JSON builder with the SHA-256 UUID chain, QR on the printed receipt, offline queue with a 24 h sync SLA plus a "late submission" dashboard, return/credit receipts, and the national-ID field at EGP 150k or more. B2B e-invoice adds signing (e-seal/USB token handled on a desktop bridge, e.g. our Electron shell) and status polling. Rough effort: several developer-months, including ETA preprod testing. The ETA SDK is public, so the cost is mostly engineering.
- Accreditation: the evidence suggests no legal requirement that a cloud software vendor be "certified" for a shop to register the software as a virtual POS/ERP. However, ETA runs a vendor certification track, and competitors advertise approval. Getting certified is recommended for sales credibility and smoother device registration. CONFIRM the exact rules and fees on pos.eta.gov.eg ("Rules and criteria for accepting the certified companies").
- Liability: the fine falls on the taxpayer (the shop), not the software vendor. Our contract and terms should still disclaim tax liability and state the shop's duty to verify submissions.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the text of ETA's "vendor certification rules" or any fee and timeline for certifying a software POS application. The portal page did not render those documents.
- The EGP 250k threshold / 31 Mar 2026 claim (single, conflicting source) is unverified.
- I did not verify the second sub-phase of phase 9 (31 Dec 2025, other governorates) against a named ETA decision. NPI says no decision naming those taxpayers was published.

## 2. VAT: rate, registration threshold, VAT on our SaaS, and the simplified regime (Law 6/2025)

### Takeaway
The standard VAT rate is 14%. Resident businesses must register once annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000, within 30 days of crossing it. Our SaaS subscriptions sold in Egypt are standard-rated at 14% once we pass the threshold. Many target shops will be on Law 6/2025's simplified regime (turnover ≤ EGP 20m): 0.4% to 1.5% turnover tax, quarterly VAT returns, and simplified bookkeeping. They stay conditional on e-invoice/e-receipt integration, which makes ETA integration a selling point.

### Cited Findings
- VAT registration is mandatory for any person whose annual taxable turnover exceeds EGP 500,000. — [PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries, Egypt Other taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/egypt/corporate/other-taxes)
- Businesses over EGP 500,000 in any 12 months must register within 30 days of exceeding it. The standard rate is 14%. — [PwC Doing Business in Egypt 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbie.pdf)
- The standard 14% rate applies to most SaaS and digital services. Since 22 Jun 2023, non-residents supplying digital services to Egyptian consumers must register under the simplified vendor registration once over EGP 500k. — [PayPro Global (aggregator)](https://payproglobal.com/saas-sales-tax/egypt/); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/vatlive/country-guides/africa-and-middle-east/egypt-vat/egyptian-vat-rates.html) says there is no threshold for non-residents (conflicts with PayPro; irrelevant if we are an Egyptian resident company)
- Standard-regime VAT registrants file monthly (Form 10). Law 6/2025 small enterprises file quarterly. — [DDD Invoices](https://dddinvoices.com/learn/e-invoicing-in-egypt); [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0577-egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises)
- Law 6/2025: in force 1 Mar 2025, for businesses with annual turnover ≤ EGP 20m, registered or not. It requires an application to ETA and is irrevocable for 5 years. Conditions: timely returns and **integration with ETA e-invoicing and e-receipts**. Benefits: turnover-based income tax of **0.4% to 1.5%** (turnover EGP 500k to 20m), exemptions (stamp/registration fees on AoA and financing, capital gains on fixed assets, dividend withholding, the withholding/advance payment system), quarterly VAT, an annual-only payroll tax return, inspections only after 5 years, and **simplified bookkeeping rules**. Exclusions: consultants with 90% or more of revenue from 1 or 2 clients, and artificial restructuring. Up to 20% over the EGP 20m cap keeps the 1.5% rate for one more year. — [EY Tax News, 28 Feb 2025](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0577-egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises); [Legal500 briefing](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/egypt/tax/new-tax-incentives-in-law-no-6-of-2025-for-small-businesses)

### Inferences
- For our customers (shops): the simplified regime lowers bookkeeping depth (no full double-entry needed for tax), but it raises the need for an ETA-integrated POS, plus simple turnover reporting and quarterly VAT summaries. Product features to emphasise: turnover-by-quarter report, VAT-by-quarter summary, e-receipt status.
- For us (the startup): until revenue crosses EGP 500k per year, VAT registration is not mandatory. After that, prices must be VAT-inclusive/14%, we issue **B2B e-invoices** to business customers through ETA (customers need them to deduct the cost), and we file VAT. We can register voluntarily earlier, so we can issue ETA e-invoices from day one (CONFIRM with a tax advisor). Our own SaaS company likely qualifies for Law 6/2025 (turnover ≤ EGP 20m). Note the 1-2 client consultancy exclusion does not fit a SaaS business.
- Pricing implication: published prices (e.g., 499/999/1,499 EGP) should state whether they include 14% VAT. Law 181/2018 requires final prices shown to consumers to include VAT (see section 5).

### Gaps
- I did not verify an ETA ruling that specifically classifies SaaS subscriptions as standard-rated services (not reduced-rate professional services). The aggregator claim is reasonable but CONFIRM.
- I found no source on whether Law 6/2025 income tax tiers have been updated since early 2025.

## 3. Personal Data Protection Law 151/2020: executive regulations, licensing, cross-border transfer, NTRA/ITIDA cloud requirements

### Takeaway
The PDPL is now fully operational. Executive Regulations (Decree No. 816 of 2025, issued 1 Nov 2025) gave a one-year grace period that ended about **1 Nov 2026, so as of today (19 Nov 2026) enforcement has begun**. Every controller and processor needs a PDPC licence (fees waived up to 100,000 records, but the licence is still required) and a registered DPO. Breaches must be reported within 72 h. Transferring or hosting data outside Egypt needs a lawful transfer basis or a PDPC licence, and violations carry criminal penalties. For us this is the single most urgent legal action item.

### Cited Findings
- Executive Regulations issued by Decree No. 816 of 2025, dated 1 Nov 2025, with a one-year grace period, so full enforcement around Oct/Nov 2026. — [Kennedys, 2026](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/). Compliance deadline 1 Nov 2026 per [Soliman, Hashish & Partners via LexisMiddleEast, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.lexismiddleeast.com/eJournal/2026-03-05_1/en).
  - **Conflict on issuer and date:** Kennedys calls it a "Prime Ministerial Decree". Al Tamimi, the Sedona Conference translation, and SHP call it a Ministerial Decree of the Minister of Communications & IT ([Legal500/Al Tamimi](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/egypt/privacy/egypts-personal-data-protection-law-(pdpl)-english-translation); [Sedona PDF](https://www.thesedonaconference.org/sites/default/files/meeting_paper/7.4%20Egypt%20Personal%20Data%20Protection%20Law%20-%20Executive%20Regulations.pdf)). DataGuidance says MCIT issued it on 25 Dec 2025 ([DataGuidance](https://www.dataguidance.com/jurisdictions/egypt)), probably the publication date. LexisMiddleEast notes it "only now been made available to everyone" (Mar 2026). Treat the grace-period end as **1 Nov 2026 at the earliest**. CONFIRM with counsel whether the clock runs from issuance or publication.
- Official Arabic text of the regulations is on the PDPC site. — [pdpc.gov.eg Executive Regulation PDF](https://pdpc.gov.eg/assets/pdf-data/Executive%20Regulation.pdf)
- The law is extraterritorial (art. 2): it applies to any processing of data of individuals in Egypt, inside or outside Egypt. — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)
- Six lawful bases (art. 6): consent, contract, legal obligation, legitimate interest, legal claims, court orders. Consent must be explicit, informed, presented **in Arabic first**, separate from T&Cs and privacy notices, and given by affirmative action. — [Kennedys citing PDPC Consent Guidelines](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/); [PDPC Consent Guidelines PDF](https://pdpc.gov.eg/assets/pdf-data/Guidelines/DSConsent.pdf)
- Licensing: PDPC licences or permits apply to controllers and processors, sensitive data, direct e-marketing, and cross-border transfer. — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)
- Licence fees (art. 19 of the regulations) are annual and scale with record count: **1 to 100,000 records: exempt**; 101k to 200k: EGP 200; up to 1m: EGP 1,000; 1.0m to 1.1m: EGP 5,000; about 2m: EGP 50,000; 5m: EGP 500,000; above 5m capped at EGP 2m over 3 years. Fees are halved if acting only as controller or only as processor. Cross-border licences cost 50% of the base fee. Direct e-marketing licences cost 10% (own marketing) or 25% (for others). A temporary permit up to 1 year is free up to 25,000 records. "A fee waiver does not waive the licence or the data protection officer." — [SOG checklist](https://sog.eg/en/blog/egypt-pdpl-website-compliance-checklist); fee table also in the [Sedona translation](https://www.thesedonaconference.org/sites/default/files/meeting_paper/7.4%20Egypt%20Personal%20Data%20Protection%20Law%20-%20Executive%20Regulations.pdf)
- DPO: the PDPC's acting CEO is quoted as saying all controllers and processors must appoint and register a DPO regardless of size. The DPO must pass a PDPC exam and be registered before any licence application. — [SOG](https://sog.eg/en/blog/egypt-pdpl-website-compliance-checklist); [Alphavito](https://alphavito.com/pdpl-executive-regulations-egypt/) (secondary sources; CONFIRM whether an outsourced DPO is allowed)
- Breach notification: notify the PDPC within **72 hours** and affected individuals within 3 working days after that. There is no risk threshold (stricter than GDPR). Processors must notify the controller without undue delay. — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)
- Cross-border transfer (art. 14): allowed only to countries with protection at least equal to Egyptian law, or with PDPC approval or explicit consent. The regulations add exceptions where the transfer is needed for a contract with the data subject, a legal obligation, vital interests, or legal claims. Transferring without approval is a criminal offence. — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)
- Penalties: arts. 35 to 40 carry criminal sanctions. Controller/processor failures (art. 38) bring EGP 300,000 to 3m. Licence breaches (art. 45) bring EGP 500,000 to 5m. — [SOG](https://sog.eg/en/blog/egypt-pdpl-website-compliance-checklist); Kennedys gives the range as "approximately 200,000 to two million" with up to 5m for some offences ([Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)). Figures differ by article; CONFIRM.
- Commentators expect a surge of licence applications and approval delays near the deadline. — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2026/egypt-s-personal-data-protection-law-the-compliance-countdown-has-begun/)

### Inferences
- Our role: for shops' end-customer data (loyalty, customer names and phones in invoices) we are a **processor**, and each shop is the controller. For our own subscriber and lead data we are a **controller**. Each shop is in principle also obliged to hold its own licence. Commercially, we should ship a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) template, Arabic-first consent text for customer capture, and data-subject request tooling (export/delete a customer).
- Cost: at launch we will hold well under 100,000 records, so the licence fee is likely EGP 0. The real costs are a registered DPO (an internal person who passes the PDPC exam, or outsourced; CONFIRM), legal drafting (privacy policy, DPA, terms), and a breach-response runbook. Rough effort: 2 to 6 weeks of founder or legal time plus counsel fees (amount unknown, see Gaps).
- Hosting outside Egypt (e.g., an EU or US VPS): the most defensible bases are "necessary to perform the contract" plus a PDPC cross-border licence (50% of the base fee, so EGP 0 under 100k records) and/or hosting in a jurisdiction considered adequate. A low-risk option is hosting customer databases in an Egyptian data centre. CONFIRM with counsel before launch, given the criminal exposure.
- NTRA/ITIDA cloud licensing: I found no evidence that a SaaS provider needs an NTRA licence to offer cloud software (NTRA licences telecom and internet services). Not confirmed (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative statement on whether a small SaaS can use an outsourced/shared DPO, or the PDPC exam fee or timeline.
- I found no list of "adequate" countries published by the PDPC.
- NTRA or ITIDA licensing for cloud or data-centre services: not researched in depth. I found no source requiring a licence for a pure SaaS application provider (CONFIRM).
- I found no data on actual PDPC licence processing times as of Nov 2026.

## 4. Company setup, ITIDA incentives, online payments (Paymob/Fawry), invoicing customers

### Takeaway
An LLC (or a one-person company, OPC) through GAFI's one-stop shop is the usual vehicle. There is no meaningful minimum capital. Formation takes days to 2 to 4 weeks until the bank account is ready, at roughly USD 1,500 to 6,000 all-in with advisors (EGP figures vary). Paymob or Fawry merchant onboarding needs a commercial register (CR), a tax card and a bank account in the same name. A sole proprietor can onboard with a national ID, tax card and bank account. Once VAT-registered, we must issue ETA e-invoices to business subscribers.

### Cited Findings
- Generally no minimum capital for an LLC. JSC minimum is EGP 250,000 (25% paid at incorporation). A branch needs EGP 5,000. — [PwC Doing Business in Egypt 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbie.pdf). A nominal LLC minimum of about EGP 1,000 is cited by [haqq.ai](https://www.haqq.ai/blog/tasees-sharika-misr). Lexcouncil cites a minimum of EGP 50,000 for foreign investors ([Lexcouncil](https://lexcouncil.com/en/insights/cost-of-setting-up-company-in-egypt)), which conflicts; CONFIRM.
- Indicative formation budget for a straightforward LLC is USD 1,500 to 6,000 (government fees, notary, advisory). GAFI steps take from the same day to a few days each. Full readiness (tax registration and bank account) takes about 2 to 4 weeks. — [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/news/company-formation-egypt) (secondary; foreign-investor oriented). Lexcouncil says USD 500 to 3,000 in direct fees for foreign investors ([Lexcouncil](https://lexcouncil.com/en/insights/cost-of-setting-up-company-in-egypt)).
- A sole proprietorship (منشأة فردية) is an alternative: register, get a tax card, and register for VAT above EGP 500k. — [Deel guide](https://www.deel.com/blog/sole-proprietorship-egypt/)
- Law 6/2025 exempts eligible small enterprises from stamp, registration and documentation fees on the Articles of Association. — [EY](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0577-egypt-introduces-tax-incentives-and-benefits-for-small-enterprises)
- Payment gateway onboarding in Egypt: sole proprietors and freelancers typically need a national ID, tax card and Egyptian bank account. Companies need a CR, tax card, bank details, beneficial-ownership documents and a signatory resolution. Names must match across CR, tax card and bank. — [XPay guide 2026](https://xpay.app/blog/documents-needed-payment-processing-egypt) (competitor gateway; general-industry claim)
- Paymob runs AML/CFT checks (e.g., suspicious transaction reports) and offers a merchant-onboarding API for platforms. — [Paymob Click T&Cs](https://paymob.com/click-by-paymob/); [Paymob Developer Portal](https://developers.paymob.com/)
- Investment Law incentives include exemption from stamp duty and notarisation/publication fees for 5 years on company contracts. Special incentives exist for some sectors and regions. — [PwC Doing Business in Egypt 2025](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/tax/documents/doing-business-guides/dbie.pdf)
- ITIDA publishes an Egypt Startup Ecosystem Report 2026. — [ITIDA report PDF](https://itida.gov.eg/English/MediaCenter/Announcements/Documents/egypt-startup-ecosystem-report-2026.pdf). I found no concrete ITIDA tax incentive applicable to a small domestic SaaS in this search (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Recommended path: an LLC or OPC, so the company name appears on the CR, tax card and Paymob account, which is better for B2B trust and e-invoicing. A sole proprietorship is cheaper and faster for an MVP but mixes personal liability with the business. CONFIRM the OPC specifics (Companies Law 159/1981 as amended in 2018) with a lawyer.
- Recurring subscriptions: confirm with Paymob or Fawry that tokenised recurring card billing is enabled for the merchant category. It is often a separate approval (not verified).
- Invoicing: before VAT registration, issue simple invoices or receipts. After VAT registration (or voluntarily earlier), issue ETA e-invoices to business customers. Shops will want them so they can deduct the subscription as a cost.

## 5. Consumer protection and e-commerce rules for selling subscriptions online

### Takeaway
Consumer Protection Law 181/2018 (executive regulations April 2019) governs distance contracting (arts. 36 to 41). It requires seller identity and contact details, clear Arabic descriptions, VAT-inclusive final prices, a 14-day no-reason withdrawal right, and a refund via the same method within 7 days. Its application to B2B SaaS sold to shops (businesses, not "consumers") is uncertain. Prudent practice is to publish Arabic terms, VAT-inclusive prices, clear renewal/cancellation and refund terms, and invoices.

### Cited Findings
- Law 181/2018 and its executive regulations (published April 2019) cover distance selling, including online stores. Sellers must show their identity and contact details, describe products clearly in Arabic, display final prices including VAT and all charges, publish a return policy, and issue invoices. — [Nilex Digital Systems](https://nilexdigitalsystems.com/articles/consumer-protection-law-egypt-online-stores)
- Consumers contracting at a distance may withdraw within 14 days of receiving the goods. Refunds go by the same payment method within 7 days of return (art. 40). Defective goods can be returned within 30 days. — [Tha Tiny Giants blog](https://blog.thatinygiants.com/en/egypt-consumer-protection-law-online-store/); [Consortio law firm translation](https://consortiolawfirm.com/egyptian-consumer-protection-law-181-2018-english-translation/)
- The CPL regulates remote contracting by internet or phone and gives a 14-day withdrawal right without reasons. — [Shalakany law firm](https://shalakany.com/new-consumer-protection-law-executive-regulations/)
- An amendment (art. 14 bis) is effective from 6 Apr 2024. — [Consortio translation](https://consortiolawfirm.com/egyptian-consumer-protection-law-181-2018-english-translation/) (content not reviewed)

### Inferences
- Whether a shop owner buying a business tool counts as a "consumer" under 181/2018 is unclear. The law is aimed at final consumers, so CONFIRM. Low-cost risk mitigation: a 14-day trial or refund window (this matches our free plan and trial), Arabic T&Cs, VAT-inclusive pricing, an easy cancellation path, and automatic-renewal notices.
- Our free plan and self-signup flow (OTP) collect personal data. The PDPL consent and notice rules in section 3 apply at signup: an Arabic consent separate from the T&Cs.

### Gaps
- I found no source on Egyptian rules for automatic subscription renewal (pre-renewal notice) specifically.
- I found no explicit authority on whether B2B SaaS buyers fall under Law 181/2018.
- E-signature (Law 15/2004) and electronic contract validity for click-through T&Cs were not researched.
